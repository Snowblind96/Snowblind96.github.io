Current Work

The Impact of Lipid Regulation in Retinal Microglia Activation and Homeostasis

My doctoral research uses the zebrafish retina to study microglial immunometabolism. I study how microglia, the resident immune cells of the central nervous system, regulate their lipid metabolism and how that in turn shapes their activation state. Microglial metabolism is plastic: it shifts upon activation, and pro-inflammatory states drive lipid synthesis and intracellular lipid droplet accumulation. My work focuses on how these droplets form, how they are regulated, and what they do to retinal function.

The retina is a perfect environment to study microglia. It has defined, accessible layers in an enclosed space, so microglia are easy to observe in vivo. It is also highly metabolic, which makes it a good system for studying the relationship between immune activation and metabolism.

💧

Lipid Droplets in the Retina

Describing lipid droplet biology in the developing retina in both retinal microglia and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, using live in vivo imaging with a dedicated lipid-droplet reporter line.

🧬

Apolipoproteins & Lipid Droplets

Determining how apolipoproteins regulate microglial lipid droplets, including the role of apoc1 (an Alzheimer's-associated gene highly expressed in microglia) in both microglial and retinal lipid handling.

🔁

Immunometabolism & Activation

Investigating how microglial activation states and lipid metabolism influence one another, and what lipid droplet accumulation signals about cell function.

Human health relevance. Lipid metabolic dysfunction in microglia is a conserved feature across several neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, and age-related macular degeneration. It is linked to impaired phagocytosis, oxidative damage, and chronic inflammation. Understanding the lipid-metabolism microglia connection in the retina could help us prevent and treat these conditions.

About

I am a PhD candidate in the MMBB program at the University of Idaho working in the Mitchell Lab. My research background spans a range of biological systems, from grizzly bears to molecular studies of microglia, and my current work focuses on the resident immune cells of the central nervous system.

The Mitchell Lab investigates the cell and molecular biology that governs microglial function during development, degeneration, injury, and regeneration, and how microglia interact with neighboring neurons and glial cells. My focus is at the intersection of neuroimmunology and metabolism. How do retinal microglia regulate their lipid metabolism, and how does that shape their activation state? My current work explores lipid droplet biology in retinal microglia and pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, and the role of apolipoproteins in these processes.
